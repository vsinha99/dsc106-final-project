The clue

One shipment.
Two records.
An incomplete picture.

Hardware leaving Taiwan appears in export records and again in import records. But the agencies can classify the same hardware differently. In our proposal’s Taiwan–U.S. example, the four hardware categories roughly agree in total while differing sharply one by one.

Epoch AI combines customs records with chip-sales estimates, accounts for re-exports, and estimates the AI-specific portion of hardware trade. These are estimated arrivals, not a census of chips running in data centers.

An H100-equivalent (H100e) expresses computing power relative to an NVIDIA H100. A chip count weights every chip equally; an H100e estimate does not. Read Epoch AI’s methodology.

What to take away

Count the chips.
Then question the count.

Trade records help us estimate where AI hardware arrives, and those estimates reveal large differences between destinations. But hardware models, trade classifications, and allocation assumptions change what those numbers mean. Therefore, compare both chip units and computing power, and read the uncertainty before treating a ranking as settled.

Behind the estimates

Sources & methods

Source: Epoch AI, Data on AI chip imports, November 12, 2026 release, downloaded November 18, 2026. The bundled destination CSV contains 3,960 rows and 32 columns: 220 destination labels across 18 quarters, from 2022 Q1 through 2026 Q2. Data credited to Epoch AI under CC BY 4.0.

How we calculate the views

The quarterly view reads the “Quarterly number of units” or “Quarterly compute capacity (H100e)” column. The cumulative view reads the corresponding “Cumulative” column at the selected quarter. We do not sum cumulative records over time. Totals sum the selected estimate once per destination. A destination’s share is its estimate divided by that total, including Unspecified; the ranking omits Unspecified as it is not a location. The time series always shows quarterly totals, even when the ranking is cumulative.

The dataset reports the EU as a bloc and contains no separate EU-member rows. We retain the publisher’s destination labels instead of regrouping overlapping regions. Blank numeric fields remain missing rather than becoming zero. Fractional chip units reflect estimates; displayed values and percentages are rounded only for readability.

What these records cannot establish

Estimated arrivals are not the same as installed or operational computing capacity. Hardware can move onward, and the accumulated series does not deduct retirement or later relocation. Model mixes within destinations are inferred. The publisher distinguishes conditional model intervals from structural alternatives and notes that some reporting, timing, and allocation errors remain uncovered. We use the exported low–high fields without assigning them a confidence level or inventing a joint distribution.

Prototype Progress and Challenges

What have you done so far?

We have built an interactive article around the same research question and Epoch AI dataset used in our proposal. We checked the destination CSV, separated quarterly arrivals from cumulative estimates, and kept the unspecified destination in the share denominator. The main D3 chart now lets readers choose a quarter, switch between chip units and H100-equivalent computing power, and inspect individual destinations. A linked dot-and-range chart shows the publisher’s uncertainty bounds, while a quarterly time series puts the selected period in context. We also added source notes, keyboard-accessible chart details, and a data table so readers can check what the visual marks represent.

What will be the most challenging part of your project to design and why?

The hardest part will be helping readers understand uncertainty without making every comparison feel meaningless. The estimates depend on customs classifications, re-export adjustments, and assumptions about which chip models reach each destination. We need to explain why a place can have a larger share of chip units than computing power while making clear that the model mix is inferred rather than directly observed. Keeping small destinations readable beside the much larger United States estimate is another design challenge, especially on phones. For the final version, we plan to test the narrative with readers and refine the annotations so that overlapping ranges and uncertain rankings are difficult to overlook.
